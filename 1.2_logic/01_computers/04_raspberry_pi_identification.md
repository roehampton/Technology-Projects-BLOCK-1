| Component | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| SoC |The SoC is the main processor of the Raspberry Pi. It performs calculations, processes instructions, and controls the overall operation of the system. |
| GPIO |GPIO pins allow the Raspberry Pi to connect and communicate with external electronic devices such as sensors, LEDs, motors, and other hardware components.|
| USB |	USB ports are used to connect external devices such as keyboards, mice, flash drives, and other USB peripherals.|
| HDMI |HDMI provides video and audio output to a monitor, television, or other compatible display device. |
| Power |	The power connector supplies electrical power to the Raspberry Pi, allowing it to operate.|
| microSD |The microSD card is used to store the operating system, software, files, and other system data. |
| Network |The Ethernet port provides a wired network connection, allowing the Raspberry Pi to access the internet and communicate with other devices. |
| Wireless |Wireless connectivity (Wi-Fi and Bluetooth) allows the Raspberry Pi to connect to wireless networks and communicate with other wireless devices. |
## Reflection
Why are many components integrated into a single board in embedded computers such as the Raspberry Pi?
Many components are integrated into a single board in embedded computers such as the Raspberry Pi to make the system compact, efficient, and affordable. Integrating the processor, memory, USB, HDMI, and networking reduces the need for additional hardware and external connections. This design saves space, reduces power consumption, and improves overall efficiency. It also makes the computer easier to install, maintain, and use. Single-board computers are suitable for various applications, including robotics, automation, education, and Internet of Things projects. The Raspberry Pi demonstrates how multiple hardware components can work together efficiently on one board while providing powerful computing capabilities in a small and convenient form factor.
